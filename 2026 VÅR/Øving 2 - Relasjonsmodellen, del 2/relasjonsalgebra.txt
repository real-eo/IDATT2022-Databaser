Notater:
σ (selection): filter rows that satisfy a predicate. Example: σ_{age > 30}(Person).
π (projection): choose columns (attributes). Example: π_{name,age}(Person).
⋈ (join): combine two relations by a condition. If no condition and attributes match, a natural join is implied; with a condition it’s a theta‑join: R ⋈_{cond} S.
− (set difference): tuples in R but not in S.
∪, ∩ : union, intersection.
⟕, ⟖, ⟗ (outer joins): extensions to RA for left/right/full outer joins (not in classic/primitive RA but common in extended RA).
ρ (rename): rename relation or attributes. Important differences between textbook RA and SQL/MySQL:
RA is set‑based (no guaranteed order); SQL results are multisets (bags) by default and can be ordered with ORDER BY.
Basic RA has no notion of NULL or three‑valued logic; SQL does, which affects predicates like IN/NOT IN.
ORDER BY, LIMIT/OFFSET, aggregates and window functions are not part of classical RA (they are extensions).
